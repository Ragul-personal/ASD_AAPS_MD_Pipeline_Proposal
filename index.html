How the verdict is computed

  1. Miscibility axis (Hansen). Hansen distance Δδ from partial parameters (δd, δp, δh) of API and polymer. Pharma rule of thumb (Greenhalgh et al. 1999, J. Pharm. Sci.): Δδ < 7 MPa½ → likely miscible; > 10 → likely immiscible. First-pass χ ≈ Vref(Δδ)²/RT is reported as a triage number only.
  2. Mobility axis (Fox + Hancock). Blend Tg from the Fox equation 1/Tg = w1/Tg,1 + (1−w1)/Tg,2 with Tg in Kelvin. Storage must sit at least 50 °C below blend Tg (Hancock, Shamblin & Zografi 1995, Pharm. Res.) for molecular motions to be suppressed over pharmaceutical timescales.
  3. Verdict. Low risk = both axes clear. High risk = either axis fails hard. Undefined = borderline on either axis. Tier 0 never issues a final answer on its own — it sets the prior and decides how much Tier-1 all-atom MD sampling the pair warrants.

Known limitations. Solubility-parameter methods systematically miss acid–base and specific interactions — a green Tier-0 verdict must still be confirmed at Tier 1. The Fox equation assumes ideal mixing of free volumes and ignores moisture-induced Tg depression (≈ 10 K per 1% w/w water for many APIs); a real deployment should carry a moisture-margin correction at 75% RH.
